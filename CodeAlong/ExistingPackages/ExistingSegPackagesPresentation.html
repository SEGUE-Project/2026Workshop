About the data

We are using open data from the Dutch statistics office CBS, and in particular 500x500 grid cells of Rotterdam. This is count-data coming from the Dutch register, as the 500m x 500m resolution (Statistiek (CBS), 2026). The data was clipped to fit the Rotterdam functional area as defined by the OECD (OECD, 2023). Municipal boundaries and waterbodies come from the kadaster (Kadaster, 2026).

Segregation Packages

There are currently (at least) 3 packages available to compute the indices of segregation we will use in this session (dissimilarity and entropy):

  • OasisR
  • divseg
  • segregation

OasisR

library(OasisR)

OasisR was deprecated last year but seems to function well again.

It provides performs most categorical & ordinal measures of segregation (inc. 2-group, 1-group, multigroup, and spatially adjusted indices). It provides resampling methods to test significance.

Its core segregation functions generally use a matrix-oriented structure: rows represent spatial units and columns represent population groups.

divseg

library(divseg)

divseg performs diversity measures and indices proposed by Massey & Denton along the 5 dimensions of segregation (Massey & Denton, 1988).

It uses a data-frame and tidyverse-oriented structure.

Group variables are selected from columns, and the package can work directly with sf objects.

segregation

library(segregation)

segregation implements D, exposure and isolation measures, and entropy-based M and H indices.

It uses a long-data structure in which the population group, spatial unit, and optional frequency or weight are represented explicitly as variables.

The package also includes inference, decomposition, and visualisation tools.

Dissimilarity index

Measures the unevenness with which two population groups are distributed across a set of spatial units. For two groups \(x\) and \(y\) distributed across \(n\) units:

\[D = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \left| \frac{x_i}{X} - \frac{y_i}{Y} \right|\]

For the first comparison, we distinguish between people aged 0–25 and everyone aged 26 and older.

Spatial Dissimilarity

Conventional D depends on the population counts in the spatial units, but not on the locations of those units relative to one another.

Rearranging the same grid cells spatially would therefore leave D unchanged.

Spatial Dissimilarity

Morrill’s spatial version of D adjusts the conventional index using contiguity between spatial units:

\[D(S) = D - \sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|w_{ij} (z_{i,g} - z_{j,g})\right|\]

where \(D\) is the traditional aspatial Index of Dissimilarity; \(z_{i,g}\) and \(z_{j,g}\) are the proportions of group \(g\) in spatial units \(i\) and \(j\), respectively; \(w_{ij}\) has a value of 1 if units \(i\) and \(j\) are adjacent or 0 otherwise.

Entropy index E

The Entropy Index measures the diversity of population groups within a spatial unit:

\[E_{i} = -\sum_{g=1}^{G} p_{ig} ln(p_{ig})\]

where \(p_{ig}\) is the proportion of group \(g\) in spatial unit \(i\), and \(G\) is the total number of population groups.

Entropy ranges from 0 (only one group present) to \(ln(G)\) (all groups have equal proportions).

Theil’s index H

Theil’s Information Theory Index H measures segregation by comparing the diversity of individual spatial units with the diversity of the overall study area:

\[ H = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{t_{i} (E - E_{i})}{TE} \]

where \(E\) is the entropy of the entire study area, \(t_{i}\) is the population of spatial unit \(i\), \(T\) is the population of the entire study area, and \(n\) is the number of spatial units.

\(H\) ranges from 0 (every spatial unit has the same composition as the entire study area - no segregation) to 1 (every spatial unit contains only one group - complete segregation).

Mutual Information index M

Another way of computing the \(H\) index is using the Mutual Information Index (\(M\)), which measures how much we know of the population group of an individual by knowing their spatial location:

\[ M = \sum_{i=1}^{n} p_{ig} L_{i} \]

where \(L_{i}\) is the “local” segregation score for unit \(i\):

\[ L_{i} = \sum_{g=1}^{G} ln \left( \frac{p_{ig}}{p_{i}p_{g}} \right) \]

\(M\) ranges from 0 (knowing someone’s location provides no information about their group membership) to E.

Entropy-based indices:

The \(H\) index is mathematically equivalent to the normalisation of \(M\) by \(E\):

\[ H = \frac{M}{E}\]
